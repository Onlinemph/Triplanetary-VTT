# ORBITAL DROP

## A Campaign Ruleset Combining Triplanetary (3rd Ed.) and Ogre (6th Ed. Revised / Battlefields)

Triplanetary handles everything above the atmosphere. Ogre handles everything below it. This ruleset is the seam between them: a multiplayer, multi-session campaign where merchants build fortunes, pirates steal them, the Patrol chases the pirates, and anyone with enough MegaCredits and cargo tonnage can put a cybertank on somebody else's colony.

**You need:** Both complete games, the Triplanetary Campaign rules (p. 14-15 of the rulebook), and the Ogre Battlefields terrain overlays. A referee is optional but helps.

---

## 1. THE CAMPAIGN LAYER

Play the Triplanetary Campaign game as written. All Campaign rules apply: free fuel, faction roles (Patrol, Pirates, Merchants, Prospectors), delivery cycles, prize ships, pirate detection, and the referee options. This ruleset changes only three things:

1. Ground units join the equipment price list and can be carried as cargo.
2. Bases have garrisons, and taking a base means winning an Ogre battle.
3. Captured bases produce income and services for their new owner.

Time runs on Triplanetary turns (one day each) until an invasion lands. Then the whole table stops, the ground battle plays out on its own clock, and the campaign resumes the next day-turn with the result applied.

### 1.01 Base income

Every base a player controls generates **MCr 0.5 per day** in addition to any scenario or faction income. This is what makes invasions worth mounting: a base is a permanent revenue stream, a fuel stop, a repair yard, and a detection post, all in one target.

### 1.02 Who can invade

Anyone. The Patrol can storm a pirate stronghold. Pirates can seize a mining base instead of just looting ships. A merchant consortium that's lost faith in the Patrol can garrison its own depot or take a competitor's. The referee (or table consensus) should treat unprovoked invasions as the political acts they are; see 8.03.

## 2. GROUND FORCES: PRICES AND TONNAGE

Ground units are equipment. They're bought at any friendly base, ride as cargo, and count against a ship's cargo capacity in tons.

| Unit                     | MCr                         | Tons                         | Notes                                                                    |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Infantry squad           | 1                           | 2                            | Specialist infantry (Marines, Engineers, HWT) cost and mass double       |
| Standard armor unit      | 5                           | 10                           | Heavy Tank, Missile Tank, GEV, GEV-PC, Crawler                           |
| Half-value armor unit    | 3                           | 5                            | Light Tank, Light GEV                                                    |
| Double-value armor unit  | 10                          | 20                           | Howitzer, Mobile Howitzer, Superheavy                                    |
| Missile Crawler (loaded) | 15                          | 30                           | Includes its Cruise Missile                                              |
| Truck / Hovertruck       | 1                           | 5                            |                                                                          |
| Light Artillery Drone    | 3                           | 5                            | Ships palletized                                                         |
| Ogre                     | 5 per armor-unit equivalent | 10 per armor-unit equivalent | See 13.03 of the Ogre rules for equivalents; ships in modules (see 2.02) |

So a Mark III (17 armor units) costs MCr 85 and masses 170 tons. A Mark V costs MCr 125 and masses 250 tons. Compare a frigate at MCr 150: ground power is cheap to buy but expensive to deliver, which is the point.

### 2.01 Convoys are the game

A transport holds 50 tons: five tanks, or a quarter of a Mark III. A serious invasion force means multiple transports moving together across the map for days, visible to detectors, escorted or not. Pirates and rival factions now have a reason to care about military shipping, and the Patrol has a reason to escort it. No item of cargo may be split between ships (Triplanetary cargo rule); Ogre _modules_ are the exception, since each module is its own cargo item (see below).

### 2.02 Ogres ship in modules

Following the Vulcan assembly rules (Ogre 15.02.2), Ogres travel disassembled. Divide an Ogre's tonnage into 50-ton modules (round the last module up); each module is a separate cargo item and may travel on a different ship. **All modules must be landed before assembly begins.** An Ogre with modules lost in transit is scrap; the surviving modules can be sold back at any base for half price.

Assembled Ogres cannot be loaded back onto ships. Getting an Ogre off a world it has conquered requires disassembly at a friendly base: one full day per 4 armor-unit equivalents.

## 3. GARRISONS

### 3.01 Free militia

Every base — planetary, asteroid, or captured — has a standing militia of **6 infantry squads** at no cost. There is never a walkover; every invasion is a battle. Militia are regular INF and are replenished automatically one day after any battle, win or lose, as long as the base stands.

### 3.02 Purchased garrison

The base's owner may buy garrison units up to a cap, at normal prices, recorded secretly at time of purchase (write the composition down, dated; the referee or a sealed note keeps everyone honest). Garrison units never appear on the space map and cost nothing to maintain.

| Base type                                                        | Armor unit cap | Infantry squad cap | Ogre allowed?                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Planetary base (Terra, Luna, Mars, Venus, Mercury, Io, Callisto) | 12             | 20                 | Yes, one, counted against the armor cap                           |
| Asteroid base (Ceres, Clandestine, emplaced surface bases)       | 6              | 10                 | Mark I or II only                                                 |
| Orbital base                                                     | —              | —                  | No ground battles; orbital bases fight by Triplanetary rules only |

Superheavies, Howitzers, and Mobile Howitzers count double against the cap; Light Tanks and Light GEVs count half, as in Ogre 1.07.

### 3.03 The reaction force

Up to **half** the purchased garrison (defender's choice at purchase time, recorded with the garrison) may be designated the reaction force. It does not set up on the map. It enters from the defender's map edge beginning on **ground turn 5**, any or all of it, on any turn from then on. This represents forces dispersed away from the base racing back when the alarm sounds.

## 4. THE INVASION SEQUENCE

An invasion happens inside a single Triplanetary day-turn, in this order.

### 4.01 Declare

During the Ordnance Phase, a player with ships in orbit over a target hexside declares invasion against the base there. Landing at an enemy base without declaring is not possible; landing anywhere else on a hostile world crashes or strands you per the normal Triplanetary landing rules.

### 4.02 Suppress

Warships in orbit over the target hexside may suppress it (Fleet Mutiny rule, adapted): a suppressing ship fires at nothing else that turn. Each suppressing warship silences the planetary defense fire of that hexside for this turn. If at least one warship suppresses, skip 4.03.

### 4.03 Run the guns

If the hexside is not suppressed, its planetary defenses fire once at **each** landing ship: 2:1 odds on the Triplanetary gunfire table, no range or velocity modifiers. A ship disabled during its landing turn crashes with all cargo. This is why you bring warships.

### 4.04 Land

Each surviving transport lands at the target hexside (normal cost: one fuel point from orbit). Its cargo is now groundside. Ships that landed are on the surface and vulnerable; they may take off on later turns only if the invader wins (the base's boosters won't serve them otherwise), so most invaders lift back to orbit empty is not an option — the ship stays down until the battle resolves. Treat landed ships as immobile D0 targets if any ground unit reaches their hex.

### 4.05 Freeze the sky

The space game pauses. All courses, ordnance, and fuel states hold exactly as plotted. No player may move, fire, or negotiate ship actions until the ground battle ends. (Table talk about the battle is encouraged. That's half the fun of pausing.)

### 4.06 Fight

Generate the map (Section 5), set up (Section 6), and play the Ogre battle to a finish.

### 4.07 Resume

Apply the aftermath (Section 7). The next Triplanetary day begins.

## 5. RANDOM MAP GENERATION

Each world has a terrain profile. Generate the battlefield when the invasion lands, not before.

**Terra, Venus, Callisto** (living worlds): One green G.E.V. map, chosen by die roll among those owned (roll for orientation too). Then roll 1d6 and place that many overlays of the defender's choice from: town, forest, swamp. Defender places overlays anywhere; overlays represent the settled country around the base.

**Mars, Luna, Mercury** (dead worlds): The orange Ogre map. Roll 1d6; the _attacker_ places that many crater or ridge overlays (attacker's choice which). Use Ogre-map rules: no stacking, ramming rules recommended for speed.

**Io** (volcanic): Orange Ogre map, 1d6 crater overlays placed by the attacker, plus 1d3 water-hex overlays placed by the defender. Water here is lava: impassable to everything, fire may cross it.

**Asteroid bases** (Ceres, Clandestine, emplaced bases): Half of any map (players agree which half before rolling). 2d6 ridge hexsides placed alternately, defender first. No GEV-type units function (nothing to hover on); they may be garrisoned or landed but sit immobile as D2 targets. Low gravity: all other units get +1 movement point.

House rule freely here. The profiles exist so every world plays differently and setup stays under five minutes.

## 6. BATTLE SETUP

1. **Defender sets up first**, secret garrison and militia revealed as counters go down. The defender owns the half of the map containing the base (place a Command Post or building to represent the base itself — a CP for asteroid bases, an Admin building with SP 20 for planetary bases). No more than 20 attack strength points may set up in the central third of the map; the rest sets up in the defender's third (adapting the Mark III Attack zones).
2. **Attacker enters** from their map edge on turn 1, all landed forces at once. The attacker chooses which edge before the defender sets up — the defender knows where the drop is coming from, not what's in it.
3. **Ogre assembly.** An invading Ogre's modules enter as a single inert marker with the attacking force. It activates after a delay of **(Size − 4) ground turns, minimum 2** (Mark III: 3 turns; Mark V: 4; Mark VI: 5). Until it activates it is a stationary target: treat all D results against it as X per the unfinished-Ogre rule (15.02.2), and it cannot fire. A defending garrison Ogre starts assembled and active. This is the invader's window of maximum danger, and a fast defender should punish it.
4. **Reaction force** enters per 3.03.
5. Use overrun or ramming rules by table agreement, but pick one campaign-wide and keep it.

### 6.01 Orbital fire support

Each warship in orbit over the battle hexside may deliver **one** strike during the entire battle, on any attacker fire phase: attack strength equal to its Triplanetary combat strength, any target, any range, resolved normally on the CRT with spillover. Ships that suppressed during landing may still strike. The defender's base counts as a building for these attacks.

A Triplanetary **nuke** may be committed instead of a battle: it devastates the hexside per the Triplanetary rules, destroying the base, the garrison, and the revenue stream. You can always break the thing you wanted to steal.

### 6.02 Victory conditions

The battle ends when one side has no units on the map (destroyed or withdrawn off its own entry edge). The attacker wins by destroying the base structure **or** by holding the map when all defenders are gone; the base is captured intact in the second case only. The defender wins by destroying or driving off every attacking unit.

## 7. AFTERMATH

**Captured base:** Changes ownership immediately. It provides fuel, resupply, boosters, and detection to its new owner starting the **next** day-turn, and income (1.01) starting the day after that. Surviving attacker units become its garrison, cap rules apply from then on.

**Failed invasion:** Surviving attacker units are captured with their landed ships unless the ships lift off — landed ships may attempt takeoff on the day-turn after a lost battle, but the base's planetary defenses fire on each (2:1, unmodified) as they rise.

**Salvage:** The battle's winner may sell captured or destroyed enemy armor at the base for 25% of list price, or repair captured (not destroyed) units to full at 50% of list. A "dead" Ogre on won ground follows Ogre 1.08.1: it counts as destroyed and is pounded to scrap; no salvage.

**Damage carries over:** A garrison Ogre that survived with damage keeps its record sheet between battles. Repair at the base costs MCr 1 per tread unit or AP gun, MCr 4 per secondary or missile rack, MCr 8 per main battery, and takes one day per 10 points spent.

## 8. FACTION NOTES

### 8.01 Pirates

A pirate raid on a base is a small invasion: same sequence, and Clandestine's special asteroids still protect the pirate home base from approach, so Clandestine itself can only fall if its location is somehow revealed (referee's call, and a fine campaign climax). Pirates raiding a prospector's automated mine fight a micro-battle: robot guards count as 2 infantry squads that cannot leave their hex, on a quarter-map, no militia. Winner takes the stockpiled ore.

### 8.02 The Patrol

The Patrol's budget (MCr 4/3/2 per the Campaign rules) now has to cover garrisons too. Suggested: the Patrol receives one free planetary-base garrison at campaign start, composition of its choice up to cap, at its home base.

### 8.03 Politics

An invasion is loud. Any faction that invades a base belonging to a player who has not attacked it first should expect consequences: the referee may raise its Patrol attention, void its docking rights at neutral worlds, or spike prices against it. If playing without a referee, agree on a standing penalty — suggested: unprovoked invaders lose base income (1.01) from **all** their bases for 5 days as markets panic.

### 8.04 Victory

Use the Campaign victory rules (net worth at an agreed end date), counting each held planetary base at MCr 300 and each asteroid base at MCr 150 toward net worth, plus garrisons at purchase value. Bases are now the biggest prizes on the board, which is exactly what makes the ground game matter.

## 9. PLAYTEST KNOBS

Three numbers to adjust after your first few sessions:

**Base income (1.01).** At MCr 0.5/day, a captured base pays off a Mark III invasion force in about half a campaign year. Raise it and the game becomes a land war; lower it and invasions become rare, dramatic events.

**Ogre assembly delay (6, step 3).** Size − 4 makes landing an Ogre a gamble. If invaders keep losing their cybertanks before activation, drop it by one turn across the board; if Ogres feel unstoppable, raise it.

**Garrison caps (3.02).** Twelve armor units on defense against a tonnage-limited attacker is stiff. If defenders win too often, remember the attacker chooses when and where to strike and can starve a base first by interdicting its shipping — the space game is the siege engine. If they still win too often, cut caps to 8/16.

## Implementation notes (this app)

Orbital Drop is playable here as a scenario — the third card on the start
menu. The space game is the real Triplanetary engine; the ground battles are
fought in the embedded Ogre view on a battlefield generated from §5's world
profiles, with the reaction force (§3.03), the module-shipped Ogre's assembly
delay (§6 step 3) and orbital fire support (§6.01) implemented in the ported
Ogre engine. Where this implementation adapts or defers the printed text:

- **Declaration timing.** Landing is an astrogation decision and astrogation
  precedes ordnance in this engine's turn, so the landings begin the day
  after the declaration rather than inside the same day-turn. The garrison
  hears the alarm a day out.
- **Suppression** uses the engine's printed Fleet Mutiny rule — a suppressed
  hexside stays suppressed — rather than §4.02's per-turn reading.
- **The base** is represented by the command post in the ground battle; the
  SP-20 admin building for planetary bases is deferred. "Captured intact"
  is read from the battle's victory level.
- **Asteroid-base invasions** (and their no-GEV, low-gravity table) are
  deferred; asteroid profiles stand their ridges in as a denser crater field.
  Overlay placement is seeded rather than chosen by the players.
- **The garrison Ogre** consumes the whole armour allowance (§3.02's "counted
  against the armor cap", read strictly); Mark III and Mark V are the types
  on sale.
- **Aftermath**: the §7 salvage credit is paid at a quarter of list; repair
  at half price, Ogre damage carry-over, module disassembly for re-shipping,
  and the failed-invasion lift-off gamble are deferred — a failed invasion's
  landed ships are simply taken.
- **The campaign economy** is base income (§1.01) over each power's opening
  treasury; the printed Campaign game's faction roles, delivery cycles and
  prize ships are not yet layered in, and victory is net worth by agreement
  (§8.04) or the knife.
